================================================================================
EVIDENCE-BASED CARE PATHWAY
PREGNANCY WITH PRIOR UTERINE SURGERY
================================================================================

PATHWAY OVERVIEW
================================================================================
This care pathway addresses the management of pregnancy in patients with prior
uterine surgery, including cesarean section and myomectomy. The pathway includes
risk stratification, surveillance protocols, delivery planning, and emergency
management for uterine rupture.

VBAC Success Rate: 60-80% (overall population)
Uterine Rupture Risk: 0.5-0.9% (low transverse scar)
Emergency Readiness: 30-minute decision-to-delivery capability required


STAGE 1: INITIAL RISK ASSESSMENT AND STRATIFICATION
================================================================================

1.1 PATIENT HISTORY REVIEW
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Required Documentation:
□ Number and type of prior cesarean deliveries
□ Incision type (low transverse, classical, T-incision, J-incision, unknown)
□ Prior myomectomy details (if applicable)
  - Number of fibroids removed
  - Location (fundal, intramural, subserosal)
  - Endometrial cavity entry (yes/no)
  - Surgical approach (laparoscopic vs open)
  - Use of monopolar electrosurgery
□ Prior uterine rupture history
□ Indication for prior cesarean (arrest disorder, fetal distress, other)
□ Prior vaginal delivery history (before or after cesarean)
□ Interpregnancy interval
□ Chronic medical conditions (hypertension, diabetes, obesity)

Diagnostic Codes:
- ICD-10: O34.211 (Low transverse scar), O34.212 (Vertical scar),
  O34.219 (Unspecified scar), O34.29 (Myomectomy scar)
- SNOMED: 416055001 (History of cesarean section), 275424008 (Previous cesarean)

1.2 PHYSICAL EXAMINATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Assessments:
□ Maternal weight and height (for VBAC calculator)
□ Blood pressure (chronic hypertension assessment)
□ Abdominal examination (prior surgical scars)
□ Pelvic examination (cervical assessment)

Labs (Initial Visit):
- CBC with differential (LOINC: 58410-2)
- Hemoglobin (LOINC: 718-7)
- ABO and Rh typing (LOINC: 882-1)
- Antibody screen (LOINC: 890-4)

1.3 RISK STRATIFICATION BY SCAR TYPE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LOW RISK (TOLAC Candidates - Level A Evidence):
• Single prior low transverse cesarean delivery
• Two prior low transverse cesareans (Level B)
• Unknown scar type without high clinical suspicion for classical
• Twin gestation with one prior low transverse cesarean
• Prior myomectomy without cavity entry and complete documentation

MODERATE RISK (Requires Enhanced Counseling):
• Interpregnancy interval <18 months (2-3× rupture risk)
• Gestational age >40 weeks
• Estimated fetal weight >4000g
• Need for labor induction
• Arrest disorder as prior cesarean indication

HIGH RISK (Relative Contraindications):
• Two or more prior cesareans
• Short interpregnancy interval (<6 months after myomectomy)
• Prior myomectomy with ≥3 fibroids removed
• Prior myomectomy with fundal/intramural location

ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATIONS (TOLAC Not Permitted):
• Prior classical (high vertical) incision - Rupture risk 4-9%
• Prior T-incision or J-incision - Rupture risk 4-9%
• Prior uterine rupture - Recurrence risk 22-100%
• Extensive transfundal surgery entering contractile myometrium
• Standard contraindications to vaginal delivery:
  - Placenta previa
  - Vasa previa
  - Active genital herpes


STAGE 2: SHARED DECISION-MAKING AND COUNSELING
================================================================================

2.1 VBAC SUCCESS PREDICTION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Calculate probability using NIH MFMU VBAC Calculator (2021 race-free version)

Input Variables and Effects:
• Prior vaginal delivery before cesarean: OR 2.38 (increases success)
• Prior successful VBAC: OR 6.48 (strongest predictor, 90-94% success)
• Treated chronic hypertension: OR 0.38 (decreases success)
• Arrest disorder indication: OR 0.55 (decreases success)
• Maternal age: OR 0.98 per year (slight decrease)
• Pre-pregnancy weight: OR 0.98 per kg (slight decrease)
• Height: OR 1.06 per cm (increases success)

Success Rates by Prior Delivery History:
- No prior vaginal delivery: 63-65%
- Vaginal delivery before cesarean: Higher success
- Prior successful VBAC: 90-94%
- Vaginal delivery before cesarean + subsequent VBAC: 93%

CRITICAL: Calculator scores inform decision-making but do NOT determine
eligibility or serve as barriers to TOLAC (ACOG guidance)

Future Pregnancy Implications:

STAGE 3: ANTEPARTUM SURVEILLANCE PROTOCOL
================================================================================

3.1 ULTRASOUND SURVEILLANCE SCHEDULE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

11-14 Weeks:
□ Cesarean scar pregnancy evaluation
□ Viability confirmation
□ Dating confirmation
CPT Code: 76801 (First trimester ultrasound)

18-22 Weeks (Detailed Anatomic Survey):
□ Fetal anatomic survey
□ Placental mapping and location documentation
□ Cesarean scar assessment
□ Initial PAS screening if placenta anterior/low-lying
  Ultrasound Markers for PAS:
  • Loss of retroplacental zone (sensitivity 82%, specificity 90%)
  • Placental lacunae (sensitivity 79%, specificity 81%)
  • Myometrial thinning <1mm (sensitivity 76%, specificity 89%)
  • Bladder wall interruption (sensitivity 46%, specificity 98%)
  • Bridging vessels (sensitivity 66%, specificity 93%)
CPT Code: 76811 (Detailed fetal anatomic examination)

28-32 Weeks:
□ Third trimester reassessment
□ Fetal growth assessment
□ Repeat PAS evaluation if initially concerning
CPT Code: 76805 (Obstetric ultrasound, detailed)

32-34 Weeks (If PAS Suspected):
□ Surgical planning ultrasound
□ MRI consideration for surgical mapping (if needed)
□ Multidisciplinary team consultation

Note: Overall ultrasound sensitivity for PAS is 91%, specificity 97%, but
absence of findings does NOT exclude PAS - clinical risk factors remain
equally important (ACOG Level B)

3.2 FETAL SURVEILLANCE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Starting at 32-34 Weeks (Individualized Based on Risk):
□ Non-stress testing weekly or biweekly (CPT: 59025)
□ Biophysical profile if indicated (CPT: 76818)
□ Fetal movement counting patient education

Increased Surveillance Indications:
- ≥2 prior cesareans
- Prior myomectomy with high-risk features
- Maternal comorbidities
- Fetal growth restriction
- Advanced maternal age

3.3 LABORATORY MONITORING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each Trimester:
□ CBC (LOINC: 58410-2) - assess for anemia
□ Blood type and antibody screen (LOINC: 882-1, 890-4)

Third Trimester (36-37 weeks):
□ Type and screen update
□ Consideration for crossmatch if high PAS risk

If PAS Suspected:
□ Kleihauer-Betke test availability (LOINC: 32140-6, 4633-4)
□ Coagulation panel
□ Blood bank notification for potential massive transfusion

3.4 SPECIALIZED CONSULTATIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Required Consultations:
□ Maternal-Fetal Medicine if:
  - Multiple prior cesareans
  - Prior classical incision
  - PAS suspected
  - Complex myomectomy history

□ Anesthesia preoperative consultation (32-34 weeks) if:
  - Planned TOLAC at facility
  - Scheduled repeat cesarean
  - High-risk features

□ Neonatology consultation if:
  - Planned early-term delivery (<39 weeks)
  - Fetal complications anticipated


STAGE 4: DELIVERY PLANNING AND TIMING
================================================================================

4.1 TIMING OF DELIVERY RECOMMENDATIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Planned Trial of Labor After Cesarean (TOLAC):
• No specific timing recommendation
• Await spontaneous labor (lowest rupture risk)
• May continue to 41+ weeks if otherwise uncomplicated
• Continuous monitoring required when in labor
SNOMED Code: 449563009 (Trial of labor after cesarean)

Elective Repeat Cesarean - Low Transverse Scar:
• Delivery at 39 0/7 - 39 6/7 weeks (Level A Evidence)
• Rationale: Neonatal morbidity increases at earlier gestations:
  - 37 weeks: 1.8-4.2× increase in respiratory/NICU/sepsis
  - 38 weeks: 1.3-2.1× increase
• Fetal lung maturity testing not routinely indicated

Elective Repeat Cesarean - Two Prior Low Transverse:
• Delivery at 38 0/7 - 38 6/7 weeks (Expert Consensus)

Elective Repeat Cesarean - ≥3 Prior Cesareans:
• Delivery at 37-38 weeks (individualized)
• Consider PAS risk in timing decision

Prior Classical Incision:
• Delivery at 36 0/7 - 37 0/7 weeks (Level B Evidence)
• Higher rupture risk necessitates earlier delivery
• Betamethasone for fetal lung maturity if <37 weeks
  RxNorm: 1514 (Betamethasone), 3264 (Dexamethasone alternative)

Prior Uterine Rupture:
• Delivery at 36 0/7 - 37 0/7 weeks
• Recurrence risk 22-100%
• Close surveillance in third trimester

Prior Myomectomy with Cavity Entry:
• Delivery at 37 0/7 - 38 6/7 weeks
• Cesarean delivery recommended (ACOG)
• 88% of providers consider cavity entry "crucial" factor

Prior Myomectomy - Extensive Transfundal:
• Delivery at 36 0/7 - 37 0/7 weeks
• Cesarean delivery recommended
• Fetal lung maturity if <37 weeks

Prior Myomectomy - Less Extensive, No Cavity Entry:
• May continue to 38 6/7 weeks
• Trial of Labor After Myomectomy (TOLAM) may be considered
• 90% vaginal delivery success rate in selected patients
• Rupture rate 0.47% (comparable to TOLAC)

Placenta Accreta Spectrum (PAS):
• Delivery at 34 0/7 - 35 6/7 weeks (ACOG/SMFM Consensus)
• Level III/IV maternal care facility required
• Multidisciplinary surgical team coordination
• Cell saver NOT recommended (amniotic fluid embolism risk)
ICD-10 Codes: O43.211-219 (accreta), O43.221-229 (increta),
O43.231-239 (percreta)
SNOMED Codes: 33058001 (accreta), 70129008 (increta), 79668009 (percreta)

4.2 MODE OF DELIVERY DECISION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TOLAC Proceeding Checklist:
□ Patient meets eligibility criteria (no absolute contraindications)
□ Informed consent completed and documented
□ VBAC calculator reviewed with patient
□ Facility capabilities confirmed:
  □ 24/7 anesthesia in-house
  □ OR immediately available
  □ Blood bank capability
  □ Neonatal resuscitation available
□ Continuous EFM capability confirmed
□ Emergency cesarean capability within 30 minutes

Scheduled Cesarean Proceeding Checklist:
□ Appropriate gestational age confirmed
□ Anesthesia consultation completed
□ Blood type and screen current (<72 hours if high risk)
□ Crossmatch if PAS suspected (4-6 units minimum)
□ Surgical plan documented
□ Neonatology notified if <39 weeks


STAGE 5: INTRAPARTUM MANAGEMENT FOR TOLAC
================================================================================

5.1 LABOR ADMISSION PROTOCOLS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Initial Assessment:
□ Review prenatal records and surgical history
□ Confirm TOLAC eligibility
□ Establish IV access (minimum 18-gauge)
□ Type and screen verification
□ Continuous electronic fetal monitoring initiation (Level B - Required)
  - External monitoring acceptable
  - Intrauterine pressure catheter NOT superior for rupture detection
□ Notify attending obstetrician of TOLAC patient
□ Confirm anesthesia and OR availability

5.2 LABOR MONITORING REQUIREMENTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Continuous Electronic Fetal Monitoring (EFM):
• Required throughout labor (Level B Evidence)
• FHR changes are PRIMARY indicator of evolving rupture
• Patterns concerning for rupture:
  - Prolonged bradycardia (most common, occurs in 33-70%)
  - Terminal bradycardia preceded by late decelerations
  - Recurrent late decelerations (87.5% of cases)
  - Variable decelerations progressing to bradycardia
  - Sudden loss of contractions on tocometry

Maternal Monitoring:
□ Vital signs every 1-2 hours (more frequently if abnormal)
□ Pain assessment (distinguish labor pain from rupture signs)
□ Abdominal examination for tenderness
□ Vaginal bleeding assessment
□ Uterine activity pattern monitoring

5.3 ANESTHESIA MANAGEMENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Epidural Analgesia - RECOMMENDED (Level A Evidence):
• May be used in TOLAC without restriction
• Does NOT mask rupture signs
• Does NOT increase failed TOLAC risk
• Does NOT result in occult rupture
• ADVANTAGE: Provides anesthesia for emergency cesarean if needed
• Standard epidural dosing and management protocols apply

General Anesthesia:
• Reserved for emergency situations
• Preferred if patient hemodynamically unstable
• 5-15 minute delay for epidural top-up may not be acceptable in rupture

5.4 LABOR INDUCTION AND AUGMENTATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ACCEPTABLE METHODS:

Mechanical Cervical Ripening (First-Line):
• Transcervical Foley catheter balloon (Level B Evidence)
• No increased rupture risk compared to spontaneous labor
• 30mL or 60mL balloon per institutional protocol
• Remove after 12-24 hours or when spontaneously expelled

Amniotomy:
• Acceptable with favorable cervix
• No increased rupture risk
• Standard indications apply

Oxytocin (Pitocin):
• Acceptable for induction AND augmentation
• Rupture risk 0.7-1.1% with augmentation
• Rupture risk 0.77-2.0% with induction
• Dose-response relationship exists with rupture risk
• Use LOWEST effective dosing
• No specific upper limit established by ACOG
• Discontinue immediately if rupture suspected
RxNorm Code: 7052 (Oxytocin)

AVOID IF POSSIBLE:

Dinoprostone (Cervidil, PGE2):
• Increased rupture risk versus mechanical methods
• Use only if benefit outweighs risk
• Enhanced surveillance if used
RxNorm Code: 3108 (Dinoprostone)

CONTRAINDICATED:

Misoprostol (Cytotec):
• CONTRAINDICATED for induction at term (Level A Evidence)
• 15.6-fold increased rupture risk
• Rupture risk up to 5.6%
• May be used for postpartum hemorrhage management only
RxNorm Code: 42331 (Misoprostol)

5.5 LABOR PROGRESS ASSESSMENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

First Stage Labor:
□ Cervical examinations every 2-4 hours
□ Document labor progress on partogram
□ Assess for arrest disorders (may predict TOLAC failure)

Second Stage Labor:
□ Standard management
□ Operative vaginal delivery acceptable if indicated
□ Avoid prolonged second stage when possible

Failed TOLAC Indicators:
• Arrest of dilation: 4+ hours with adequate contractions
• Arrest of descent: 3+ hours (nulliparous) or 2+ hours (multiparous)
• Non-reassuring fetal status
• Maternal exhaustion or request
• Signs of uterine rupture

Conversion to Cesarean:
□ Notify OR team immediately
□ Continue EFM during transport
□ Anesthesia assessment (epidural top-up vs general)
□ Target decision-to-delivery <30 minutes
ICD-10 Code: O66.41 (Failed attempted VBAC)
CPT Code: 59618 (Global package for failed TOLAC requiring cesarean)


STAGE 6: EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT OF UTERINE RUPTURE
================================================================================

6.1 CLINICAL RECOGNITION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY INDICATOR - Fetal Heart Rate Abnormality:
• Present in 33-70% of ruptures
• May be ONLY sign in 50% of cases
• High suspicion patterns:
  - Prolonged bradycardia
  - Terminal bradycardia with preceding late decelerations
  - Sudden severe variable decelerations
  - Recurrent late decelerations

MATERNAL SIGNS (Sensitivity Varies):
• Acute abdominal pain between contractions: 58-66%
• Uterine tenderness: 79-81%
• Cessation of contractions: 52-61%
• Vaginal bleeding: 15-45% (often absent)
• Loss of fetal station: Pathognomonic when present
• Easily palpable fetal parts abdominally: 42%
• Maternal hemodynamic instability (late finding)

Rupture Risk by Scenario:
┌────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────┐
│ Clinical Scenario                  │ Rupture Risk     │
├────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────┤
│ Spontaneous labor (TOLAC)          │ 0.4-0.52%        │
│ Oxytocin induction                 │ 0.77-2.0%        │
│ Oxytocin augmentation              │ 0.9-1.4%         │
│ Prostaglandin + oxytocin           │ 1.4-2.2%         │
│ Misoprostol (contraindicated)      │ Up to 5.6%       │
│ Classical/T-incision scar          │ 4-9%             │
│ Two prior cesareans                │ 1.8-3.7%         │
│ Elective repeat cesarean (baseline)│ 0.03%            │
└────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────┘

ICD-10 Codes: O71.1 (Rupture during labor), O71.00-03 (Before labor onset)
SNOMED Codes: 34089005 (During labor), 63487001 (Before labor)

6.2 EMERGENCY RESPONSE PROTOCOL
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TARGET: Decision-to-Delivery ≤30 minutes (RCOG/NICE/ACOG)
OPTIMAL: 10-18 minutes for best outcomes

STEP 1: RECOGNITION AND ALERT (0-2 Minutes)
□ Recognize warning signs
□ Activate emergency response ("Code OB" or institutional protocol)
□ Immediate notifications:
  □ Senior obstetrician/attending
  □ Anesthesiologist
  □ OR team (activate)
  □ Neonatology/pediatrics
  □ Blood bank
  □ Additional nursing support
□ Invoke massive obstetric hemorrhage protocol if indicated

STEP 2: INITIAL MATERNAL RESUSCITATION (Concurrent)
□ ABCDE approach
  A - Airway assessment
  B - High-flow oxygen 15L/min via non-rebreather mask
  C - Establish TWO large-bore IV access (14-16 gauge)
  D - Rapid assessment of consciousness
  E - Exposure and examination
□ Left lateral tilt (or manual uterine displacement)
□ DISCONTINUE OXYTOCIN IMMEDIATELY
□ Vital signs q5min minimum
□ Consider terbutaline 0.25mg subcutaneous (temporizing only)
  RxNorm Code: 10395 (Terbutaline)

STEP 3: FLUID AND BLOOD RESUSCITATION
□ Crystalloid resuscitation:
  - Warmed lactated Ringer's or normal saline
  - Up to 2L rapidly (then reassess)
□ Activate MASSIVE TRANSFUSION PROTOCOL
  - O-negative blood if type-specific not immediately available
  - Crossmatch 4-6 units PRBCs minimum
  - Maintain 1:1:1 ratio (PRBC : FFP : Platelets)
  - Anticipate up to 10-15 units in severe cases
□ Labs: CBC, type and screen/crossmatch, coagulation panel, fibrinogen
□ Foley catheter (monitor urine output, bladder decompression)

STEP 4: SURGICAL INTERVENTION
□ Emergency cesarean delivery
  - General anesthesia preferred if unstable
  - Epidural top-up if stable and adequate level achievable quickly
  - Spinal if no existing epidural and patient stable
□ Neonatal resuscitation team ready
□ Intraoperative assessment:
  - Locate rupture site
  - Assess extent (complete vs incomplete)
  - Evaluate for:
    • Fetal expulsion into abdomen
    • Extension to cervix or vagina
    • Broad ligament hematoma
    • Bladder involvement
    • Vascular injury

STEP 5: SURGICAL REPAIR VS HYSTERECTOMY DECISION

Consider PRIMARY REPAIR if:
• Patient hemodynamically stable or stabilizing
• Small, clean rupture edges
• Single rupture site
• Transverse direction
• No cervical or vaginal extension
• No broad ligament involvement
• Patient desires future fertility
• Adequate hemostasis achievable

Consider HYSTERECTOMY if:
• Intractable hemorrhage despite uterotonics
• Multiple rupture sites
• Longitudinal tear extending to cervix
• Broad ligament hematoma with active bleeding
• Bladder injury requiring extensive repair
• DIC developing
• Unable to achieve hemostasis with repair
• Patient hemodynamically unstable despite resuscitation

STEP 6: HEMORRHAGE MANAGEMENT

Uterotonic Agents:
□ Oxytocin 10-40 units in 1L crystalloid, wide open
  RxNorm: 7052
□ Methylergonovine (Methergine) 0.2mg IM
  Contraindication: Hypertension
  RxNorm: 6847
□ Carboprost (Hemabate) 250mcg IM q15-90min (max 8 doses)
  Contraindication: Asthma
  RxNorm: 19750
□ Misoprostol 800-1000mcg rectally
  RxNorm: 42331

Tranexamic Acid:
□ 1g IV over 10 minutes, may repeat once
  Give within 3 hours of delivery for maximum benefit
  RxNorm: 202585

Surgical Hemostasis Techniques:
□ Uterine artery ligation
□ O'Leary sutures (uterine/utero-ovarian vessels)
□ B-Lynch compression suture
□ Bakri balloon tamponade
□ Interventional radiology (if time permits and patient stable)

6.3 POST-RESUSCITATION MANAGEMENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Immediate Postoperative:
□ ICU admission if massive transfusion or hemodynamic instability
□ Serial hemoglobin q4-6h × 24h
□ Coagulation panel monitoring
□ Urine output monitoring (Foley to remain)
□ Vital signs q15min × 4h, then q1h × 24h
□ DVT prophylaxis when hemostasis achieved

Neonatal Outcomes Monitoring:
• Fetal mortality (developed countries): 2-6%
• NICU admission (survivors): 23-40%
• Assess for HIE, birth trauma, need for therapeutic hypothermia

Maternal Complications:
• Hysterectomy requirement: 14-80% (severity-dependent)
• Severe PPH: 43%
• Maternal mortality (developed countries): 0.1%

6.4 DOCUMENTATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Required Documentation:
□ Time of symptom recognition
□ Time of emergency activation
□ Decision-to-delivery interval
□ Surgical findings (rupture location, size, direction, involvement)
□ Repair vs hysterectomy and rationale
□ Estimated blood loss
□ Transfusion products and quantities
□ Neonatal resuscitation details and Apgar scores
□ Complications

CPT Codes:
- 59610-59620: VBAC-related delivery codes
- 59525: Cesarean hysterectomy (add-on)


STAGE 7: POSTPARTUM CARE AND FOLLOW-UP
================================================================================

7.1 IMMEDIATE POSTPARTUM CARE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Vaginal Birth After Cesarean (Successful):
□ Standard postpartum care
□ Monitor for bleeding (slightly higher risk than primary vaginal)
□ Endometritis surveillance (2.9% rate)
□ Hospital discharge 24-48 hours if uncomplicated
SNOMED Code: 10761961000119103 (VBAC delivery)
CPT Codes: 59610 (global), 59612 (delivery only)

Failed TOLAC (Cesarean After Labor):
□ Enhanced PPH monitoring (3.2% transfusion risk)
□ Endometritis prophylaxis and surveillance (7.7% rate)
□ Hospital stay 48-72 hours typical
□ Pain management (multimodal approach)
□ Assess for wound complications

Elective Repeat Cesarean:
□ Standard cesarean postpartum care
□ Hospital stay 48-72 hours
□ Wound care and infection surveillance
□ VTE prophylaxis per institutional protocol

Post-Rupture Management:
□ ICU level care initially if indicated
□ Serial lab monitoring until stable
□ Wound care (may have vertical incision)
□ Psychological support and counseling
□ Lactation support (may be delayed)

7.2 HOSPITAL DISCHARGE PLANNING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Discharge Criteria:
□ Hemodynamically stable × 24 hours
□ Pain controlled with oral medications
□ Tolerating diet
□ Voiding spontaneously
□ Ambulating independently
□ No signs of infection
□ Lochia appropriate
□ Adequate social support at home

Discharge Instructions:
□ Wound care (if cesarean)
□ Activity restrictions (6-week recovery for cesarean)
□ Warning signs (fever, excessive bleeding, wound issues)
□ Contraception counseling
□ Follow-up appointment scheduling
□ Lactation support resources

7.3 INTERPREGNANCY INTERVAL COUNSELING
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Critical for Future Pregnancy Planning:

OPTIMAL: ≥18-24 months between delivery and next conception
• Allows complete uterine healing
• Lowest rupture risk in subsequent pregnancy

AVOID: <6 months
• Highest complication risk
• Elevated uterine rupture rate
• Associated with adverse outcomes

COUNSEL: 6-18 months
• Increased risks but not absolute contraindication
• Enhanced surveillance in next pregnancy
• After myomectomy: 87% of providers consider <6 months crucial risk

Contraception Options:
□ LARC methods (IUD, implant) - highly effective
□ Combined hormonal contraception (if no contraindications)
□ Barrier methods
□ Permanent sterilization if family complete

7.4 POSTPARTUM VISIT (6 WEEKS)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Assessments:
□ Wound healing (if cesarean)
□ Cervical and uterine involution
□ Psychological screening (postpartum depression, PTSD if rupture)
□ Contraception review and provision
□ Future pregnancy counseling
□ Chronic condition management

Future Pregnancy Counseling:
□ PAS risk increases with each cesarean:
  • 1 cesarean: 0.24% baseline, 3% with previa
  • 2 cesareans: 0.31% baseline, 11% with previa
  • 3 cesareans: 0.57% baseline, 40% with previa
  • ≥4 cesareans: 2.13-6.74% baseline, 61% with previa
□ TOLAC remains option after 2 low transverse cesareans
□ Early ultrasound screening for placentation in future pregnancy
□ Optimize health before next conception:
  - Weight management
  - Chronic condition control (hypertension, diabetes)
  - Nutritional status
  - Smoking cessation if applicable

EVIDENCE QUALITY GRADES
================================================================================

LEVEL A EVIDENCE (Highest Quality):
• Most women with 1 prior low transverse cesarean should be offered TOLAC
• Epidural analgesia may be used in TOLAC
• Misoprostol contraindicated for induction in scarred uterus
• Elective repeat cesarean at 39 weeks for single prior low transverse

LEVEL B EVIDENCE:
• Continuous electronic fetal monitoring required during TOLAC
• Two prior low transverse cesareans may be candidates for TOLAC
• Mechanical cervical ripening (Foley) acceptable for TOLAC induction
• Prior classical incision delivery at 36-37 weeks
• Ultrasound findings alone do not exclude PAS

EXPERT CONSENSUS:
• Cesarean recommended for myomectomy with cavity entry
• PAS patients should deliver at Level III/IV centers (ACOG/SMFM)
• Two prior cesareans: deliver at 38 weeks
