[GOALS]
GOAL-001:
  Description: Maintain maternal free T4 at upper limit of trimester-specific normal range
  Target Value: Free T4 at 90-100% of upper limit
  Target Days: Throughout pregnancy
  Priority: HIGH
  Guideline: ATA 2017 Guidelines - prevents fetal hypothyroidism from overtreatment

GOAL-002:
  Description: Prevent maternal thyroid storm during pregnancy and delivery
  Target Value: No thyroid storm events
  Target Days: Throughout pregnancy and immediate postpartum
  Priority: HIGH
  Guideline: Maternal mortality 8-25% if thyroid storm occurs

GOAL-003:
  Description: Identify and monitor fetuses at risk for thyrotoxicosis
  Target Value: TRAb monitoring per protocol, monthly ultrasound if elevated
  Target Days: 18-22 weeks initial, 28-32 weeks if high TRAb
  Priority: HIGH
  Guideline: Fetal thyrotoxicosis occurs in 1-5% of TRAb-positive mothers

GOAL-004:
  Description: Transition from PTU to methimazole to minimize hepatotoxicity
  Target Value: Complete transition by 16 weeks gestation
  Target Days: 12-16 weeks gestation
  Priority: HIGH
  Guideline: PTU hepatotoxicity can be fatal; methimazole preferred after organogenesis

GOAL-005:
  Description: Screen and manage at-risk neonates for thyrotoxicosis
  Target Value: Cord blood TRAb, TSH/T4 at days 3-7 and 10-14
  Target Days: Birth through 14 days
  Priority: HIGH
  Guideline: Neonatal thyrotoxicosis has 12-20% untreated mortality

GOAL-006:
  Description: Achieve euthyroid state before conception or early pregnancy
  Target Value: TSH within trimester-specific range
  Target Days: Preconception or first trimester
  Priority: MEDIUM
  Guideline: Uncontrolled hyperthyroidism increases preeclampsia, preterm birth

GOAL-007:
  Description: Reassess and adjust therapy postpartum for immune rebound
  Target Value: Thyroid function testing at 6-12 weeks postpartum
  Target Days: 42-84 days postpartum
  Priority: MEDIUM
  Guideline: Graves' disease flares in 3-12 months postpartum

[CLINICAL CONTENT]

--- OVERVIEW ---
Hyperthyroidism affects 0.1-0.4% of pregnancies and requires carefully orchestrated management across all trimesters to optimize maternal and fetal outcomes. The cornerstone of treatment involves using propylthiouracil (PTU) during the first trimester due to methimazole's teratogenic risk, then switching to methimazole after 16 weeks to avoid PTU hepatotoxicity. Treatment targets should maintain maternal free T4 at the upper limit of normal rather than normalizing TSH, which helps prevent fetal hypothyroidism from antithyroid drug overexposure. Modern management has reduced perinatal mortality from historical rates of 20-45% to approximately 1.7% when disease is well-controlled.

--- PATHOPHYSIOLOGY ---
Understanding normal pregnancy thyroid physiology is essential for accurate diagnosis. Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) shares structural homology with TSH at its beta subunit, allowing hCG to weakly stimulate the TSH receptor. As hCG peaks between 8-14 weeks gestation, this causes physiologic TSH suppression and mild elevation in free T4. Pregnancy induces a 50% increase in thyroxine-binding globulin due to elevated estrogen, which increases total T4 and T3 concentrations while free hormone levels remain relatively stable. The thyroid gland enlarges by 10-30% and approximately doubles its hormone production.

While thyroid hormones cross the placenta minimally, TSH receptor antibodies (TRAb) freely cross as IgG molecules, potentially causing fetal or neonatal thyrotoxicosis. Antithyroid medications also cross freely and can treat fetal hyperthyroidism but may cause fetal hypothyroidism if doses are excessive.

--- RISK FACTORS ---
The ATA and ACOG recommend aggressive case-finding rather than universal screening. Women with any of the following risk factors should undergo TSH testing at the first prenatal visit:
- Personal history of thyroid disease, thyroid surgery, or radioiodine therapy
- Family history of thyroid disease, particularly autoimmune conditions
- Type 1 diabetes mellitus or other autoimmune disorders
- Age greater than 30 years
- History of miscarriage, preterm delivery, or infertility
- Presence of goiter or thyroid nodules
- Symptoms suggestive of thyroid dysfunction (tremor, palpitations, heat intolerance, unexplained weight loss)
- Multiple gestation or hyperemesis gravidarum
- Women with prior Graves' disease remain at risk even after thyroidectomy or radioiodine ablation because TRAb can persist for years

--- DIAGNOSIS ---
The diagnosis of hyperthyroidism in pregnancy requires TSH measurement using trimester-specific reference ranges. Practical trimester ranges when laboratory-specific ranges are unavailable: First trimester 0.1-2.5 mU/L, Second trimester 0.2-3.0 mU/L, Third trimester 0.3-3.5 mU/L.

Overt hyperthyroidism is defined as TSH suppressed below the trimester-specific lower limit with elevated free T4 or free T3. Subclinical hyperthyroidism involves suppressed TSH with normal free thyroid hormone levels and notably does not require treatment during pregnancy.

The distinction between Graves' disease and gestational transient thyrotoxicosis (GTT) fundamentally changes management. Graves' disease requires antithyroid drug therapy, while GTT is self-limiting and requires only supportive care. TRAb testing is essential: TRAb-positive indicates Graves' disease, TRAb-negative suggests GTT. GTT affects 1-3% of pregnancies, occurs with extremely high hCG levels (typically >200,000 IU/L), and resolves spontaneously as hCG declines in the second trimester.

--- TREATMENT ---
First trimester: Propylthiouracil is the only recommended antithyroid drug. Methimazole exposure during weeks 5-10 carries 1.6-4% absolute risk for specific congenital anomalies including aplasia cutis, choanal atresia, esophageal atresia, omphalocele, and facial dysmorphism. PTU dosing: mild disease 50-100mg TID, moderate 100-150mg TID, severe 200-300mg TID (maximum 450mg/day).

Second and third trimester: At 12-16 weeks gestation, switch from PTU to methimazole to minimize PTU hepatotoxicity risk (25-50% fatal in severe cases). Conversion ratio approximately PTU:methimazole = 20:1. Methimazole administered as 5-15mg once daily for most patients.

Treatment target: Maintain free T4 at upper limit of normal rather than normalizing TSH. If TSH normalizes during treatment, this indicates potential overtreatment and risk of fetal hypothyroidism.

--- FETAL MONITORING ---
Fetal risks: Poorly controlled maternal hyperthyroidism substantially increases complications. Intrauterine growth restriction shows odds ratio of 4.57 compared to euthyroid pregnancies. Additional fetal risks include tachycardia (>160 bpm), goiter, thyrotoxicosis, hydrops fetalis, and stillbirth.

Fetal ultrasound surveillance should begin at 18-22 weeks for all women with elevated maternal TRAb, current antithyroid drug therapy, or history of Graves' disease. Monthly ultrasound from 22 weeks should assess fetal thyroid size, heart rate, growth parameters, bone maturation, and amniotic fluid volume.

--- NEONATAL CONSIDERATIONS ---
Neonatal thyrotoxicosis occurs in 1-5% of infants born to TRAb-positive mothers, with untreated mortality of 12-20%. The condition typically lasts 2-3 months, correlating with TRAb clearance (half-life 1-2 weeks). Presentation includes irritability, tachycardia, poor weight gain despite voracious appetite, diarrhea, goiter, and exophthalmos. Symptoms may be delayed 1-10 days if the mother was taking antithyroid drugs.

Testing protocol for at-risk neonates: cord blood TRAb if mother TRAb-positive, TSH/free T4/free T3 at days 3-7 and 10-14, continue weekly until TRAb normalizes.

--- COMPLICATIONS ---
Thyroid storm: Thyroid storm in pregnancy carries 8-25% mortality and affects approximately 1-2% of hyperthyroid pregnancies. Common precipitants include labor, cesarean section, preeclampsia, infection, and diabetic ketoacidosis.

Emergency treatment must begin immediately: PTU loading 500-1000mg then 200-250mg every 4 hours; iodine 1-2 hours AFTER PTU (Lugol's solution 10 drops every 8 hours); dexamethasone 2mg IV every 6 hours for 4 doses; propranolol 60-80mg every 4-6 hours; ICU admission with supportive care. Delivery should be avoided during active thyroid storm—stabilize the mother for 24-48 hours before delivery if possible.

Maternal complications: Uncontrolled hyperthyroidism significantly increases preeclampsia (odds ratio 1.78), preterm birth (odds ratio 1.81, absolute risk 19.3% vs 12.3% baseline), low birth weight (9x higher if uncontrolled), and ICU admission (odds ratio 3.70).

--- POSTPARTUM MANAGEMENT ---
The postpartum period represents immune rebound from pregnancy-related immunosuppression, with Graves' disease flare risk highest 3-12 months after delivery. Postpartum thyroiditis affects 5-10% of all postpartum women and carries approximately 50% risk of permanent hypothyroidism.

Reassess thyroid function at 6-12 weeks postpartum. Differentiate postpartum thyroiditis (TRAb-negative, self-limiting) from Graves' disease recurrence (TRAb-positive, requires treatment). Women with Graves' disease typically require increased antithyroid drug doses postpartum due to immune rebound.

--- BREASTFEEDING ---
Both PTU and methimazole are compatible with breastfeeding per ATA and AAP guidelines. PTU is safe at doses ≤450mg/day with only 0.025% excreted in milk. Methimazole is safe at doses ≤20-30mg/day. No routine monitoring of infant thyroid function is required. Propranolol is the preferred beta-blocker during breastfeeding. Radioactive iodine is absolutely contraindicated during breastfeeding.
